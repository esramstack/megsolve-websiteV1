The opportunity

01 · CONTEXT

UCSF Benioff Santa Rosa was operating on ageing and failing desktops and laptops, causing delays and mismanagement across the site. A full technological overhaul was commissioned.

The project then hit an internal setback. Initial scoping by a field services engineer allocated significantly fewer hours than the work required, overlooking the imaging process for all 400 hardware units entirely. Left unaddressed, that gap would have delivered new hardware into the estate without the standard build on it.

Correcting the scope introduced both complexity and urgency. The objectives remained: upgrade hardware, improve communication tools, prioritise safety, optimise space, standardise software, improve display quality, and integrate it all inside a fixed budget and a tight timeline.

The solution

02 · DELIVERY

A field approach built around the constraint that mattered most: this was a working hospital, and clinical operations came first.

PH 01

Team organisation

  • Team divided by expertise so each member worked to their strengths
  • Skills and resources mapped against the corrected scope
PH 02

Night-time working model

  • Critical installation tasks scheduled overnight to avoid disrupting hospital operations
  • Quieter, more controlled working environment achieved during peak-risk tasks
PH 03

Separated staging and installation

  • Dedicated rooms assigned to staging and to installation to prevent overlap
  • Concurrent workstreams enabled without interference
PH 04

Parallel installation

  • New work model introduced running installation simultaneously across multiple PCs
  • Overall installation time reduced significantly against sequential working
PH 05

Automation and consolidation

  • Automation techniques applied to streamline warehouse operations across the large hospital site
  • Staging and imaging consolidated into a single room for all 400 PCs
  • Waste removal executed off-peak, at night, to minimise interference

The impact

03 · OUTCOME

Problem resolved and estate delivered

  • Time-wasting technical issue investigated, root-caused, and resolved
  • All 400 units staged, imaged, and installed to standard

Operations protected

  • Automation and single-room consolidation improved organisation and workspace optimisation
  • Hospital daily operations continued uninterrupted throughout

Honest accounting

  • Project ran to 3.5 months, beyond the initial estimate
  • Completion cost approached $50,000, exceeding the scoped budget by 30 to 40%, absorbed against estimated profit
  • Lessons in scoping accuracy and financial management captured for future engagements

Knowledge retained

  • Project data stored in the CRM system for reverse engineering on future work
  • Client trust maintained through transparency and delivery despite the internal setback
